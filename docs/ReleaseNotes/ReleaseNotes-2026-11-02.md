# Release Notes — 2026-11-02

Every user's vault can now be used as a plain store, with a depository record of what is theirs, and an optional protected account mode delays payouts to the owner's own wallet by two to five days so a stolen key does not mean stolen funds. With that mode on, an owner's own withdrawal ends paid, queued or refused, never silently held. The connected app carries both: a deposit and withdraw card and a vault protection page that states what it could not read as unknown. Separately, every state-changing entry point anyone may call is now recorded with what backs it, and the pre-deploy gate refuses an open entry point nobody recorded. The contract changes reach the testnets only with the all-waves fresh deploy; the keeper passes for protected accounts arrive later, disarmed.

## Contracts — every permissionless step is recorded with what backs it (PR #3098)

The owner's rule is that every permissionless step that keeps the protocol moving has the platform keeper as its backstop. Until now nothing recorded which open entry point was which, so a new step could ship with no caller and stall silently. The new ledger, `contracts/permissionless-steps.json`, lists every state-changing entry point anyone may call on the Diamond and on the contracts shipped beside it, and says what backs each one: a keeper pass and its arming flag, a keeper pass still in review, another committed service (the sanctions delister), another route to a step already backed, the party it serves, a one-time initializer, a caller restriction stated in words for review, or a recorded gap with the issue that decides it.

Whether an entry point is open is read from the compiler's syntax tree, not claimed. A function is caller-gated when, before it can finish, it reverts unless the caller is a particular address, holds a role or appears in a registry. The reading follows modifiers, internal helpers (with the caller passed in), virtual overrides (so an upgradeable contract's owner-only upgrade is seen as gated), if/else branches and early exits only an allowed caller can take. It does not follow external calls, whose caller is the contract itself; it does not count a comparison of the caller with an address the caller passed in (a role renunciation's confirmation, a protected-account setter named by its own account), since every caller can pass its own address; and it treats a KYC requirement as a screen rather than a gate, because it admits everyone while enforcement is off. Of 797 state-changing entry points, 622 are caller-gated and 175 are open; all 175 are classified.

The pre-deploy gate runs the full check as step 1e, reading the same syntax-tree build as the measured-delivery and loan-terms guards. A keeper row's sending file must carry the function's name as a string literal in code; a comment, a backticked mention or an ABI fragment's name does not count. The keeper's typecheck runs the keeper half on its own, so a keeper change that stops sending a recorded step, or renames its pass or flag, fails there too. A keeper-pending row is refused once the keeper already sends the call, so a merged pass must update its row.

Seventeen steps are recorded as gaps (staging continuations and unwinds, the daily NFT-rental deduction, the backstop fill, tier-exclusion refresh, expired-intent and expired prepay-listing teardown, claim consolidation, stale-sale teardown, the sanctions-flag refresh and reward-entry compaction). Each needs an owner call between a keeper backstop and the party it serves; that is #3097. Closes #2984.

> Caveat: a caller restriction the reading cannot see is recorded as "gated" with a written reason; eight such rows exist (signature-relayed risk settings, the delisting veto, the governance timelock's executor role), and a reviewer audits them rather than the check.
> Caveat: when the delivery-proof pass is retired (#3064) and the delivery-conflict and protected-account passes merge (#2999, #3090), their ledger rows must change in the same change; the check fails until they do.

## Thread — Protected account mode and the depository vault in the app

The connected app now carries the depository vault and protected account mode that the contracts introduced. The vault page gains a deposit and withdraw card for every asset kind. The figure it calls free is the contract's own (tracked holding less whatever loans, offers, intents and queued payouts hold), so the card never offers more than the contract will release. With protection on, the withdraw button says before signing that the withdrawal is queued and for how long.

A new vault protection page shows the account's settings, the queued payouts and position NFT transfers with their request codes, the changes waiting the delay, the payout addresses and the recovery signers. It covers locking, unlocking, co-signing, releasing, cancelling and moving the account to a new wallet. A recovery signer can open another account from the same page to accept the role and act on it. Whatever cannot be read is stated as unknown: a queue that could not be read is never shown as empty, and the page shows no controls at all when the settings themselves could not be read.

The desk, the accept flow, the fee-tariff check and the vault's locked figure now read the contract's free figure too, so a queued payout is never counted as spendable. The Terms gate keeps every write that frees the user's own assets or protects the account open to a wallet that has not accepted new Terms, including asking to unlock.

The new copy is translated into all nine shipped languages.

## Thread — Protected account mode and the depository vault

Every user's vault can now be used as a plain store with no loan involved. A user may deposit an ERC-20, ERC-721 or ERC-1155 asset from their own wallet and withdraw what is theirs to any address. What is theirs is a separate depository record: what they deposited or adopted and payments to them that stayed in the vault, less what they withdrew or spent. A balance the protocol keeps in a vault for a loan, an offer or a claim (a rental prepayment, a lent or pledged NFT, proceeds owed to a position holder) never enters that record, so it can never leave this way. The contract also lists every asset that has entered a user's record, so the app finds a deposit no loan or offer names. Many such balances carry no lien, because only VPFI had a user-facing exit before; bounding the withdrawal by its own record closes that whole class at one boundary instead of liening each balance. A vault now accepts an NFT delivered by another Vaipakam vault, read from the Diamond's vault registry, so a protocol move of an NFT between two users' vaults (a default to the lender, an NFT paid to a protected account) no longer fails at the receiving vault; any other sender is still refused. The tracked ledger that bounds every payout from a vault now covers all three asset kinds under one key, not ERC-20 balances alone. No payout by any route can take more than the vault's tracked holding of that asset, and collateral sold through a marketplace listing now leaves that holding when the sale fills.

Protected account mode is a new optional setting per account. It exists so that a stolen wallet key does not mean stolen funds. With it on, every payout from the vault to the owner's own wallet waits a delay of two to five days, chosen by the owner. That covers the owner's withdrawals and every protocol settlement that would pay the owner's wallet from their vault. Payments the protocol makes to the owner's wallet from elsewhere are credited to the vault instead, including a payment moved straight from a counterparty's wallet such as a borrower's partial repayment, and payments to counterparties are never delayed. Payouts may only go to the owner or to up to five allowlisted addresses. Up to five secondary signers, each accepting from its own key, may cancel payouts, co-sign them and lock the account. A lock cancels everything queued and blocks new payouts and new positions, including taking over a loan position by a sale or an obligation handover. It never blocks loan servicing, and a settlement payout to a locked owner stays in the vault instead of failing. A majority of three or more signers may move the account to a new wallet after the delay; from then on only the new wallet manages the account, including its lender-intent capital, its auto-extension reserve, its open offers, its lock and its pin, and the old wallet and its keepers lose every right over it, spending its vault VPFI on perks or unstaking it included. The new wallet also acts as the holder of the positions the old wallet still holds (an in-place extension and its consent, for example), is never paid a keeper reward for doing so, and the old wallet's earlier extension consent stops counting. The new wallet cannot be one of the account's signers, so no wallet both manages the account and co-signs its payouts. Every loosening change waits the delay and can be cancelled meanwhile; a lock, a replacement or a switch-off voids every allowlist and signer change still pending (a replacement drops the old wallet's pending setting changes too), and a matured addition expires if it is not applied within one further delay. Position NFTs of a protected owner can move only through a queued, cancellable transfer that follows the payout rules (allowlist and co-signing), and while that protection is on a lender position cannot be sold. A lock also cancels a replacement the owner proposed. A collateral or parallel-sale listing by a protected account may pay marketplace fees only to the owner or an allowlisted address, cannot be posted while the account is locked or replaced, and is checked again at every fill.

An admin pause that lasts three days without a break now opens an escape route (an automatic pause fired during it does not restart the count): every user may take what is theirs from their own vault to their own wallet, and queued payouts keep releasing. A user may also pin their vault to its current implementation (a vault behind the current one is upgraded first, so the pin names the code it runs), so only they can upgrade it; with protected mode on, the pin comes off only through the delayed unpin. Staked VPFI and rewards paid into the vault count as the owner's deposits, so a replacement wallet can withdraw them.

The old stuck-ERC-20 recovery, which withdrew an untracked balance straight to the wallet, is replaced by adoption. The owner declares the source of an untracked asset of any kind and signs an acknowledgement. The asset is credited to the tracked holding without moving, then leaves only through an ordinary withdrawal, so the delay covers it too. The sanctions screen of the declared source, the ban and its automatic lifting carry over unchanged.

The per-token ledger replaces the old ERC-20 balance without migrating it, so it reaches a chain only by a fresh deploy. Every in-place refresh script now refuses a Diamond from before it. Keepers gain a lock permission bit used for a lock command from the owner's linked chat. The off-chain passes that release due payouts, apply due setting changes and act on lock commands come in a separate keeper change, disarmed. Until then a due payout is released by its owner or anyone else calling the release. Each new event shows in the owner's activity feed, and a signer or nominee also sees the rows that name them.

> Caveat: the old tracked ERC-20 balances are not migrated; a chain takes the new ledger only by fresh deploy.

## Protected account mode — an owner's own withdrawal never ends held (PR #3095)

With protected account mode on, every payout from a vault passes the payout gate. When the account is locked, or its queue of waiting payouts and transfers is full, the gate keeps the amount in the vault instead of queuing it. A settlement relies on that: it must never fail because of the payee's account state. Until now, though, two routes the owner starts went through the same gate: the VPFI withdrawal and the release of surplus loan collateral. On a locked account or a full queue, either one reported success while paying nothing and queuing nothing.

Each of those routes now asks the same question first, and refuses when the answer is no. The depository withdrawal already refused in this case. So an owner's own withdrawal ends one of three ways: paid, queued or refused. Settlements keep the hold.

The rule lives in one place in the protected-account library, and each voluntary route calls it. The collateral release checks it only when the release goes to the account's own manager. A release to another holder of the position never passes the gate.

This is fresh-deploy material, like the rest of protected account mode.
