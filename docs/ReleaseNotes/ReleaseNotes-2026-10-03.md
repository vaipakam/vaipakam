read are now staged and completed rather than abandoned, a complete in-place

## Thread — transport epochs 3b-ii-A2-i: a deferred claim day is staged, not abandoned (PR #2308)

Until this change, a claim day that more epochs listed than one read could scan was deferred with nothing drawn: the coverage the window had found was released back to the epochs, and the next attempt started over against the same live batches. Now such a day keeps what it found. The window's coverage moves into a staging record for that claimant's day — debited from the epochs, credited to no one — and anyone may continue staging the rest from where the last scan stopped, reserve the day's residual from the live sources once staged transport plus the live sources can cover it, resolve the record page by page, and be paid on the last page. Nothing settles before that last page: the day pays out, persists and exhausts its epochs only then, and until then nothing is half-paid. A record that stalls expires on a deadline sized in preparation calls from the work its day's list needs, and past it anyone may unwind it, returning exactly what it held; the claimant may cancel earlier. A record whose committed entries changed lifecycle since its reservation — forfeited, defaulted, liquidated, expired, or closed before the day — is stale: its first resolution page unwinds it instead of resolving it, it is never paid on the split it committed, and anyone may unwind it at once with no deadline to wait for; the settlement that now applies then proceeds afresh. A record that has begun resolving is beyond any deadline and only completes.

Every value movement is explicit. An epoch's staged components are recorded on the epoch and named in an event with the record; the live recycled residual and, page by page, the epoch legs are held in a dedicated custody row against the record; fresh and the cap headroom are reserved by count — the live fresh row's era ledger records every debit as a spend, so fresh is never held out of it — with the figures written on the record so the unwind reverses each source exactly. Reserved headroom reads as encumbered, never as paid: a concurrent one-call settlement sees less pool, less delivered headroom, less recycled runway and less loan-side cap, and defers rather than treating the reservation as a payout — so a loan side another holder's record has reserved is never trimmed against the reservation, and an entry's expiry clock does not run through an interval in which its owner's claim would defer on one. A record stages exactly what a claim may draw: an attested delivery's epoch whose membership is whole and that lists only the day being settled. An unattested delivery's epoch is neither drawn nor staged, and a record remembers each one it passes and re-checks it before any live source is reserved, so an attestation landing after the scan is never lost. An epoch listing more than one day is withheld until the contested-allocation machinery lands; a record's scan passes it without counting it as pending, so a day holding many of them never fills the record's page of re-checks. When a record gives staged value back to an epoch — by unwinding, or by returning what it staged beyond what its reservation assigned — the return is logged for the day and announced, and every other record standing on that day re-offers the epoch before it may reserve anything from the live sources: a record that scanned the epoch while it was drained would otherwise reserve live funding past it. The record's own scan is the only scan that advances its place in the day's list: the read that opened it records none, so the record's first preparation rescans that read's window, one page, and disposes of every epoch it passed. Whoever indexes a delivery may name its predecessor in a day's late chain as well as in the day's list, so a chain deeper than the bounded walk covers can still be joined in constant work. A record's scan offers each epoch once, even when a restarted chain walk meets an epoch its re-checks already offered, and a restart counts its own pages in the record's deadline. What a record has reserved of the live fresh is unavailable to the reconciliation moves as well — the compensation demotion and the fresh-to-recycled correction leave it in the row and on the delivered ledger — so a resolving record's last page can always charge and pay what it reserved. The reservations themselves, and what each source has available net of them, are published by the rewards lens, so an operator can see why a settlement defers and how much custody stands committed; a record's committed entries with their reserved slices, and the epochs it passed over pending, are readable page by page beside its epochs. A claim that names where it is to be delivered carries that venue into any record it opens, so the staged part is delivered where the paid part was. The claimant's expiry clock does not run while a record holds their day. A record that passes more pending epochs than it tracks is prepared no further and can only be unwound, so it references nothing more for a day it can never settle. The in-place refresh retires the earlier shapes of the entries whose signatures changed. The public reward preview reports nothing at all while a reservation would make the claim defer, the legacy window's part included, as the claim itself pays nothing. Reward custody cannot be activated while a staging record is resolving, since a resolving record's consumed epoch value rests in the Diamond's balance under no attribution until its last page pays it; the record's remaining pages are anyone's to drive, and it only completes. The cooldown a release puts on an obligation is published — in an event at the release and through a read on the epoch view — so a claim that opens no record during it is never an unexplained silence. A record's page of remembered pending epochs is reconciled before it is enforced, so a full page that frees places by re-checking does not overflow on the epochs it takes in the same scan; and the preview's availability test counts a forfeited legacy entry's fresh once, as the claim does. A record asks its day's epochs for no more fresh than the pool could ever pay, so a pool that shrinks while a record scans stops further staging without stopping the scan. The custody activation ceremony reads the count of resolving records before it sends anything, and the epoch view publishes that count; the lens publishes the live fresh available beside the reserved; and a cooldown that has passed reads as none. Every restart of a record's late-chain walk adds the pages that walk needs to the record's deadline, and every preparation forced after the record had finished scanning — by a member the day's list gained, a restoration, or a passed epoch that became stageable — adds one; at least one each, never rounded into another's page, so a rescan forced on a finished scan always lengthens the lease by the call it costs, however few items it walks, while a preparation with nothing new to scan adds nothing. What staging records have reserved of the recycled runway is out of every mover's reach on the recycled side as well: a remittance plans from the runway net of it, a repatriation and a reattribution may take only what is fundable net of it, and a settlement can never consume below it — three figures that each carried their own copy of the commitment subtraction now read one. The expiry clocks read the same fresh room the claim's gate reads, so a reservation of the live fresh pauses them as it defers the claim. A record's resolution and unwind consume its epochs in the order the plan staged them and drop each as they go, so a consumption budget that runs out partway spends the epochs the plan ranked first and returns the rest, and closing a record that accumulated many epochs over many preparations never costs more than one page: the epochs a record holds are not kept inside the record itself. Value in staged form is an earmark of the Diamond's own balance while reward custody is inactive, so no other claim can spend what a record is holding: the share a page returns to its epoch leaves that earmark with it, and the share a page consumes stays in it until the last page pays the claimant. The earmark is published beside the reservations, so the figure that lowers the available balance is never unexplained. And a record holding a reservation blocks every change of posture, not only the custody activation: a reward-role change moves the delivered allowance the record's payout will charge, so it waits for the record to settle or unwind — both of which anyone may drive.

To make room for this on the settle path, the claim's entry walk, the expiry sweep's walk and the forfeit sweep's walk moved off the facets that inlined them onto three hosts reached by one self-call each; the facets keep their facades and their own accounting, and the staging lifecycle has a facet of its own with its reads beside the epochs' other reads. Scope: standalone claim days. A sweep's day, a multi-chunk domain and the legacy slice are the next slice (#2305). Refs #1566, #1956, #2305, #2296; design in #2297.

A staged day whose claimant is flagged by the sanctions oracle, whether before its resolution starts or between its pages, is not paid. Reward VPFI that has not yet reached a flagged wallet is forfeited: it goes into the recycle bucket alongside the day's treasury forfeit, and an event names the record, the claimant and the amounts. The forfeited share is charged to no loan side's reward cap, as no forfeit is, and the record's payout event claims no delivery venue for it: a delivery that did happen is announced on its own, with the venue it actually reached. VPFI already sitting in the claimant's vault stays frozen there. Because a forfeit cannot fail, a flagged claimant can no longer leave a record stuck in mid-resolution. This applies to rewards only; loan proceeds owed to a flagged party keep the existing wind-down freeze. A compensation demotion on a deployment without reward custody now also leaves untouched whatever a staging record has reserved of the delivered ledger, as the custody-activated path already did.

## Thread — a complete in-place refresh now removes every function the code no longer has (PR #2385)

When a function's signature changes, the Diamond gains the new function, but the old one keeps running the code it was last installed with until something removes it. The testnet in-place refresh used to remove these only from hand-kept lists, one per past incident, so any retirement nobody added to a list stayed callable. Base Sepolia carried eleven such functions through its complete refresh on 3 October:
- older shapes of the offer-accept entry points;
- two keeper-approval setters from before keeper permissions changed shape;
- four reward and acknowledgement receive hooks.

All eleven still ran pre-refresh code against current data. The deployment record also omitted the seven addresses they pointed at (#2313).

The refresh now works the other way round. After it installs the current functions and runs its one-time migrations, it asks the Diamond what it routes. It then removes every function outside the current deploy's set, names each one and where it pointed before it goes, and checks that each is gone. The hand-kept removal blocks are deleted. The one block kept is a migration that uses its retired function as a "not yet done" marker, and it removes that function itself. A Diamond that already routes exactly the current set sends no removal at all.

This matters for the reward-custody activation. The refresh records the Diamond's routing as it stands, and activation is refused on any other routing. Base Sepolia's record, taken on 3 October, included four retired reward receive hooks. Those are exactly the kind of route the record is meant to rule out, and the record cannot detect them: it proves only that nothing changed after it was taken. A refresh with this change removes them first, then records the routing.

The rule depends on the refresh's list of facets being complete. A facet missing from that list would now have its live functions removed, rather than left on old code as before. The existing deploy check that pins the refresh's function set to a fresh deploy's is what prevents that.

Base Sepolia is unchanged by this PR. Clearing its eleven routes and taking a new routing record needs one more refresh run under a pause. That run is the operator's decision.

## Thread — A testnet drawdown rehearsal now moves the pool and the venue with the price feed (PR #2372)

Rehearsing a health-factor drop or a liquidation on testnet means moving a faucet asset's price. The obvious way, moving only its mock price feed, does not work. The faucet's mock trading pool keeps its old price, and the oracle stops trusting a pool whose price disagrees with the feed by more than 3%. Past that point the asset reads as illiquid, which looks exactly like a pool too shallow to trade. #2314 was first diagnosed as a depth problem for that reason. It was the rehearsal method, not the pool.

There is now one script for repricing a faucet asset, and it moves everything that carries the price: the price feed, the pool's price, and the price the mock swap venue pays out in a liquidation. These are three separate transactions, not one atomic change, because each mock belongs to a fixed owner wallet. For a few blocks in between, the asset can briefly read as illiquid. If a run stops part-way, running the same command again finishes it. It derives the pool's new price with the same calculation that set the pool up in the first place; the two scripts now share it, so they cannot drift apart. It works on the two faucet tokens that have their own feed (tLIQ and mUSDC). It refuses mWETH, whose feed is shared with WETH: moving it would move the quote side of every faucet pool.

Before sending anything, the script refuses in these cases:

- The deployment record no longer matches the chain.
- The sender does not own the mocks.
- The swap venue is not registered and enabled on the Diamond. (Registered is not the same as chosen: another enabled venue could still handle a liquidation, and the report names any.)
- The Diamond would not read the new price, or the venue would not pay it.
- The asset would read as illiquid even after the full move.
- The feed is not the one the Diamond actually reads. The script checks this in a dry run: it nudges the feed up, or down if needed, and confirms the Diamond's price follows.
- The pool is not the one the oracle actually reads. The script checks this in a dry run too: it empties the pool and confirms the asset turns illiquid.

The script has no switches for skipping any of this.

After the move, the script also reports, without refusing, the swap venue's other settings and the venue's price for every liquid faucet asset, compared with the oracle's. These decide what a liquidation actually pays, and the script does not write them. It also says what it does not inspect: how much of the payout token the venue holds, the venue's price for any token outside the faucet set, and the illiquid faucet tokens, which a liquidation hands over as they are rather than selling. It names any other swap venue the Diamond could send a liquidation to, whose payout it does not inspect. It reports a venue that anyone could call, because the venue holds funds, and a token whose decimals differ from the repriced asset's, because the venue pays out on raw amounts. A clean run is therefore not a promise that a liquidation will settle correctly.

A companion rehearsal runs the script against a local copy of the live testnet, playing the real mock owner, and checks the outcome before anyone touches the shared testnet. The Base Sepolia runbook has a new section covering both and reminds operators to restore the seeded price afterwards. On testnet the script has not yet been run by anyone; it needs the mock owner's key.

Closes #2314.

## Thread — Base Sepolia refreshed to current main; #2355's refinance fix is live there (PR #2379)

The Base Sepolia Diamond now runs current main (`f8176ea06`). Its last refresh was in mid-September. Every facet the deployment script installs was replaced or added in place, and the vault template, the reward custody holder and the proxy implementations were updated with them. Two kinds of facet were not touched: the facet that performs upgrades, which is installed when the Diamond is first created, and the old implementations behind retired functions, which an in-place refresh never removes. The Diamond address did not change. The deployment record for Base Sepolia, and the consolidated copy the apps and workers read, now describe the live Diamond.

The refresh was needed for #2355's contract half: a lender can now accept a borrower's refinance request directly even while automatic matching is switched off. #2355 changed four facets and a library shared between them, so replacing the refinance facet alone would have left the Diamond running mixed versions of that shared code. The live bytecode of every facet #2355 touched now matches main exactly.

The refresh ran as the documented two-run rollout. Base Sepolia was paused from 14:41 to 15:15 UTC on 2026-10-03:
- The first run replaced the facets and backfilled the chain's reward role (canonical).
- The second run, under a pause the new code counts, performed the one-time rebase of the fresh-reward paid counter. It was rebased to zero, on the operator's declaration that this chain has no fresh-reward payout history; the counters read zero under that pause.
- The full pre-deploy regression ran first. It found 18 test-harness failures, fixed separately with no contract change.
- Before the refresh, the stored-data layout was checked: it has only grown at the end since the last refresh, so existing records read the same.

Three things are stated rather than implied:
- **Fresh-funded reward payouts are refused on Base Sepolia until the separate reward-custody activation ceremony runs.** On the canonical chain the new code lets fresh rewards pay out only up to what custody has received, which is nothing until it is funded. Any claim or remittance with a fresh part is therefore refused. A claim or remittance funded entirely from recycled VPFI is not affected by that limit: it still pays from the Diamond's own balance, the same path it used before this refresh. This is a consequence of shipping main, not of #2355, and the ceremony needs its own operator answers.
- Selectors retired from the code over time are never removed by an in-place refresh, so eleven of them still route to old implementations (tracked in #2313). Every current function routes to the new code.
- The first attempt stopped mid-broadcast on a transient RPC error, after the pause and some implementation deployments but before any facet change. It rewrote the deployment record with addresses that were never put into service. The record was restored before the rerun, and the gap is tracked in #2377.

The refinance fix is proven on the deployed code, not only by bytecode comparison. On a fork of the live Diamond, with automatic matching switched off on the fork only, a lender's direct accept of a refinance request completes the refinance. The same scenario against the Diamond as it stood before this refresh fails exactly as the fix intended to stop it failing. That check also found that a refinance of a loan backed by illiquid collateral cannot complete on the current code, because the post-refinance risk check has no branch for illiquid collateral the way loan opening does. That is a separate, older defect, recorded in #2380 for a decision.

## Thread — The connected app downloads a quarter of the contract data it used to (PR #2392)

Every connected session downloads a description of the platform's contract: which functions exist, and which errors and events it can report. The 2026-10-03 live review found this file had grown to 2.8 MB, about three and a half times its size in July. That was recorded as UX3-008.

The cause was repetition, not growth. The description was assembled by joining each part of the contract's own description. Each part repeats the errors and events it shares with the others, and three quarters of the file was exact copies.

The description is now built once, when the contract data is exported, with the exact copies removed: 0.65 MB instead of 2.8 MB. A removed copy is identical to the entry kept, so nothing the app, the indexer or the keepers can read or decode has changed. The indexer's own coverage checks report the same events before and after.

A check now fails the build if the combined file falls out of date, or if a part the export produces is missing from the list the combined file is built from. The combined file can no longer drift from the parts that are exported. The export builds the combined file before publishing anything, so if an exported part is unlisted the export stops with nothing changed, rather than publishing new parts beside an old combined file. Which parts are exported at all is still a hand-kept list; a part left off that list is not caught by these checks.

## Thread — The Rate Desk, Offer Book and core screens in plain words, usable by newcomers (PR #2378)

The owner asked for the app to be easy for someone with no trading or crypto background, including the advanced tools. This change rewrites the most jargon-heavy surfaces and makes the advanced ones approachable, following the 2026-10-03 live review (`docs/FindingsAndFixes/Findings20261003-LiveUiUxReview.md`, UX3-007, 012 and 013).

The Rate Desk no longer speaks like a trading terminal. Choices that read "GTC", "AON" and "IOC" now read "Until I cancel", "Only all at once" and "Short-lived". "Quoted mid", "spread", "asks", "bids" and "fills" become the middle rate, the gap between offers, offers to lend, requests to borrow and agreed loans. Each choice on the offer form now explains what the selected option means in a visible line. Those explanations used to sit only in hover tooltips, which a phone never shows. A short "How this page works" guide opens on a first visit and folds away after. The note shown to Basic-mode visitors now invites them in and points to the step-by-step pages, where it used to read as a warning to stay away.

The Offer Book now shows how much collateral each offer involves, not just which token. When the collateral is recorded as illiquid, the card says it would be handed over as-is if the loan is not repaid. It never calls collateral liquid just because nothing marked it otherwise. Choosing which side to show and how to sort is now available to everyone; only filtering by raw token address stays in Advanced mode. A lender offer that can be taken in part now states its collateral as the figure for the full offer, since taking part of it needs proportionally less; and when a token's details can't be read, the card still shows the recorded amount in raw units instead of dropping it. Someone else's offer that cannot be taken from this page (part of it already taken, or an NFT as the loan or its collateral) now says why on its card, instead of showing a label with nothing to click. On the Rate Desk, when the offers come from a saved copy because the live read failed, the chart's description of the middle rate says so too, since a phone can show the chart without the offers list that carries that warning.

Showing amounts on Activity rows was attempted and withdrawn: rebuilding them in the app from raw event data kept producing wrong cases (batched transactions, NFT claims, range offers), and the right place to record them is the indexer, which already understands each event — that is now tracked separately. The Alerts card now says when no Telegram link was made from this browser, and that it cannot see a link made on another device. Across the app, "principal" reads "loan amount", interest "builds up" rather than "accrues", and the commonest "on-chain" phrases read "on the blockchain".

Every changed label and the new guide are translated in all ten supported languages, and the guide quotes each language's own button and heading names. Every rewritten sentence is retranslated too, not only the renamed labels — an earlier draft kept older translations whose vocabulary was exactly the jargon being removed, so the plain-language change had reached English only. The automated browser tests and the live testnet drivers were updated to the new labels in the same change.

## Thread — The app states what it knows about loans, claims and discounts, on every surface (PR #2373)

A live review of the connected app on 2026-10-03 found that several screens either said something untrue or left out a figure another screen already knew. This change fixes those. The review itself is recorded in `docs/FindingsAndFixes/Findings20261003-LiveUiUxReview.md`.

A lender opening a loan the borrower had already repaid saw a card headed "If this loan is not repaid", saying the app was "still checking" whether the loan could be closed out. It stayed there for as long as the page was open, two rows below a line confirming nothing was owed. The checks that card waits on are deliberately switched off for a settled loan, so the answer could never arrive. The card now retires as soon as any source shows the loan is settled. A settled loan cannot reopen, so that source can only be ahead, never wrong. For a loan that closed normally, the "what happens next" line is no longer coloured as a warning.

On the VPFI discount page, the tier table said a holder of exactly 20,000 VPFI gets the top 24% tier. The protocol gives that holder the 20% tier, because the top tier starts strictly above 20,000. The table now states every boundary the way the protocol applies it, in plain words, and relabels itself when the language changes. The explanation of how the discount is earned also described a single "30-day average". The real rule is three checks: a few days of continuous holding, a recent average where recent days can count more, and a cap at the lowest level the holding has reached over its own history (up to 30 days). The page now says so, and says plainly why a top-up does not raise the discount straight away. Both texts are updated in all ten translated languages.

The claims page already showed the exact amount each claim pays. The collect-everything checklist, the loan page's claim button and the positions list did not. All of them now state the same figure from one shared source. A positions row with money waiting also offers to collect it. While the amount is still loading, or if it cannot be confirmed, the loan page says so rather than leaving it out; the claim itself stays available, since it pays whatever is owed on chain. When the app confirms there is nothing to collect, it says that instead and does not offer the button. A lender on a fallback-settled loan now sees their own share, not the whole collateral, labelled as what is recorded now: claiming first tries to match the loan with another one, and a match pays the loan's asset instead, so the page says the payout may change and repeats that at the moment of signing. A borrower whose loan was repaid by a swap that left something over now sees that amount too, including when it is the only thing to collect: such a claim used to be missing from the claims page entirely, and the loan page's own pre-check refused it. Collateral added during a failed liquidation, which the claim pays as a separate transfer in a different asset, is listed as well, and every part of a claim — borrower's or lender's — stays listed while its details load instead of being replaced by another part. A lender's recovery that is an NFT is named beside any proceeds held for them, and those held proceeds now show their amount (they are paid in one asset, so the old "held proceeds" placeholder hid a figure the app could state). A token whose details cannot be read is now said to be unreadable rather than shown as loading indefinitely, and the confirmation keeps a checking or unconfirmed state instead of replacing it with a generic description. Positions rows with a claim waiting show the loan's live status. The payout follows the loan's live status, so a loan the chain shows as repaid is no longer described as a default recovery while the index catches up, and a confirmation opened while the claim was being checked stands down if there turns out to be nothing to collect. The loan page now reads that one loan's claim on its own, rather than scanning every position the wallet has held. On a defaulted loan, the lender's claim now says what the recovery is: a cash amount, the collateral itself, or partly held in other assets. It does not compute a shortfall. The app does not know what the loan owed at the moment of default, and an earlier draft of this change, which compared against the loan's current principal, would have told a holder who bought the position how much they "lent".

The floating Support button covered page content wherever it sat: on phones, tablets and narrow desktop windows alike. It is gone. Support is now an entry in the navigation, in the side menu on wide screens and the More menu on phones. The phone header showed only a green dot for the network; it now shows the network's name, taking the room from the app name beside the logo, which stays readable to screen readers.

Not in this change: a plain-language pass over the core journeys and the advanced tools, and a screen for withdrawing surplus collateral, which the protocol supports and the app does not yet expose. Both follow in later PRs.

## Thread — Borrowers can take back collateral their loan no longer needs (PR #2389)

The protocol has always let a borrower withdraw collateral from an open loan, as long as the loan stays healthy. The app never offered it. A borrower whose collateral had grown in value had no way to use the surplus short of closing the loan. The 2026-10-03 live review recorded this as UX3-009.

A loan page now has a "Take back extra collateral" section for the borrower, in Basic mode as well as Advanced. It states how much can be taken back right now, using the protocol's own live limit, and refreshes that figure while the page is open. It also says plainly that taking collateral back makes the loan riskier, and that a figure right at the limit can be refused if prices move before the transaction lands. A Max button fills in the whole limit.

When nothing can be taken back, the page explains why, as far as the app can tell: the loan needs all of its collateral, or the collateral cannot be priced at the moment. When the app cannot tell which, it says so. If the limit could not be read at all, the page says that instead of showing zero.

Before the wallet opens, the app checks again for each thing that would make the protocol refuse, and says the reason in plain words:
- the wallet is no longer the borrower-position holder;
- a sale listing of the lender's position is linked to the loan;
- a swap-to-repay order is pending against the loan (the page says this app cannot cancel it);
- the limit has moved below the amount.

When the collateral is VPFI, the page notes that the fee-discount tier may drop; when it can't tell whether the collateral is VPFI, it says so and gives the same caution. A swap-to-repay order that passed its deadline without filling still blocks the withdrawal until it is cancelled, and the page says so. If any of the checks can't be answered — the sale-listing check or the swap-order check — nothing is sent and the page says it couldn't check. The limit is shown rounded down, so typing the figure the page states is always within it. An amount with more decimal places than the token has is refused rather than rounded, and the confirmation shows the exact amount sent. A zero limit is explained only once the loan is confirmed open — until then the card says it couldn't confirm the loan's status. When the deployment is paused, the card says so; when it couldn't check whether the deployment is paused, or whether a sale listing is linked, it says that beside the amount instead of presenting the withdrawal as certain. A loan status or position owner whose latest check failed is treated as unknown rather than as its last answer, and if the token's details can't be read the card stays and says so. Switching networks or accounts closes an open confirmation, the amount can't be edited while a confirmation is open, and the confirm button is disabled if the limit falls below the reviewed amount. While a refinance request is open on the loan, the card explains that taking collateral back would make every lender's acceptance fail, and refuses. That check also catches a request posted from another tab after the page loaded; a request made on another device can't be seen yet, and the card says so. A check that fails before the wallet opens is reported as a check, not as a failed transaction. The card disappears once the live loan status shows the loan is no longer open, even before the lists catch up. Like repaying, taking back extra collateral works for a wallet that hasn't accepted the current Terms, because it only returns the borrower's own assets.
