## Thread — Protected account mode and the depository vault

Every user's vault can now be used as a plain store with no loan involved. A user may deposit an ERC-20, ERC-721 or ERC-1155 asset from their own wallet and withdraw what is theirs to any address. What is theirs is a separate depository record: what they deposited or adopted and payments to them that stayed in the vault, less what they withdrew or spent. A balance the protocol keeps in a vault for a loan, an offer or a claim (a rental prepayment, a lent or pledged NFT, proceeds owed to a position holder) never enters that record, so it can never leave this way. The contract also lists every asset that has entered a user's record, so the app finds a deposit no loan or offer names. Many such balances carry no lien, because only VPFI had a user-facing exit before; bounding the withdrawal by its own record closes that whole class at one boundary instead of liening each balance. A vault now accepts an NFT delivered by another Vaipakam vault, read from the Diamond's vault registry, so a protocol move of an NFT between two users' vaults (a default to the lender, an NFT paid to a protected account) no longer fails at the receiving vault; any other sender is still refused. The tracked ledger that bounds every payout from a vault now covers all three asset kinds under one key, not ERC-20 balances alone. No payout by any route can take more than the vault's tracked holding of that asset, and collateral sold through a marketplace listing now leaves that holding when the sale fills.

Protected account mode is a new optional setting per account. It exists so that a stolen wallet key does not mean stolen funds. With it on, every payout from the vault to the owner's own wallet waits a delay of two to five days, chosen by the owner. That covers the owner's withdrawals and every protocol settlement that would pay the owner's wallet from their vault. Payments the protocol makes to the owner's wallet from elsewhere are credited to the vault instead, including a payment moved straight from a counterparty's wallet such as a borrower's partial repayment, and payments to counterparties are never delayed. Payouts may only go to the owner or to up to five allowlisted addresses. Up to five secondary signers, each accepting from its own key, may cancel payouts, co-sign them and lock the account. A lock cancels everything queued and blocks new payouts and new positions, including taking over a loan position by a sale or an obligation handover. It never blocks loan servicing, and a settlement payout to a locked owner stays in the vault instead of failing. A majority of three or more signers may move the account to a new wallet after the delay; from then on only the new wallet manages the account, including its lender-intent capital, its auto-extension reserve, its open offers, its lock and its pin, and the old wallet and its keepers lose every right over it, spending its vault VPFI on perks or unstaking it included. The new wallet also acts as the holder of the positions the old wallet still holds (an in-place extension and its consent, for example), is never paid a keeper reward for doing so, and the old wallet's earlier extension consent stops counting. The new wallet cannot be one of the account's signers, so no wallet both manages the account and co-signs its payouts. Every loosening change waits the delay and can be cancelled meanwhile; a lock, a replacement or a switch-off voids every allowlist and signer change still pending (a replacement drops the old wallet's pending setting changes too), and a matured addition expires if it is not applied within one further delay. Position NFTs of a protected owner can move only through a queued, cancellable transfer that follows the payout rules (allowlist and co-signing), and while that protection is on a lender position cannot be sold. A lock also cancels a replacement the owner proposed. A collateral or parallel-sale listing by a protected account may pay marketplace fees only to the owner or an allowlisted address, cannot be posted while the account is locked or replaced, and is checked again at every fill.

An admin pause that lasts three days without a break now opens an escape route (an automatic pause fired during it does not restart the count): every user may take what is theirs from their own vault to their own wallet, and queued payouts keep releasing. A user may also pin their vault to its current implementation (a vault behind the current one is upgraded first, so the pin names the code it runs), so only they can upgrade it; with protected mode on, the pin comes off only through the delayed unpin. Staked VPFI and rewards paid into the vault count as the owner's deposits, so a replacement wallet can withdraw them.

The old stuck-ERC-20 recovery, which withdrew an untracked balance straight to the wallet, is replaced by adoption. The owner declares the source of an untracked asset of any kind and signs an acknowledgement. The asset is credited to the tracked holding without moving, then leaves only through an ordinary withdrawal, so the delay covers it too. The sanctions screen of the declared source, the ban and its automatic lifting carry over unchanged.

The per-token ledger replaces the old ERC-20 balance without migrating it, so it reaches a chain only by a fresh deploy. Every in-place refresh script now refuses a Diamond from before it. Keepers gain a lock permission bit used for a lock command from the owner's linked chat. The off-chain passes that release due payouts, apply due setting changes and act on lock commands come in a separate keeper change, disarmed. Until then a due payout is released by its owner or anyone else calling the release. Each new event shows in the owner's activity feed, and a signer or nominee also sees the rows that name them.

> Caveat: the old tracked ERC-20 balances are not migrated; a chain takes the new ledger only by fresh deploy.
