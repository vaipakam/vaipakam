# The next app can borrow: take a lender's offer whole, with every figure shown

The Borrow page of the next connected app opens. A borrower chooses what to borrow and what to lock, from the tokens the network's deployment record suggests or by pasting a token address, and each token's class is read live from the protocol: priced and liquid, illiquid (valued at zero and handed over as-is on default), or a price the protocol currently distrusts, which closes new loans on it. A pasted address carries a note that no token-safety screening runs on this network. The pair is remembered per network in the browser only.

The page then lists the lender offers open for that pair, read from the protocol's own index through the wallet's connection (or the public read path without a wallet). Each offer is written as the borrower would live with it: the amount at the lender's floor rate for the term, the collateral asked, the loan-to-value at today's prices or why it cannot be worked out, the interest mode, partial repayment, any interest schedule and what a missed period costs, the expiry, and what the borrower would receive after the initiation fee. An offer the protocol would refuse says why, in its own terms, and offers no button. Offers the page cannot take yet (borrow requests, NFT legs, the reader's own) are counted, never silently dropped, and the order (takeable first, then lowest rate, then larger amount) is stated as an order, not advice.

Taking an offer opens the full review before the wallet: what arrives in the wallet, the fee and how it is set (including the small share of it the protocol pays back to whoever sends the acceptance, which on a direct take is the borrower), the amount locked in the borrower's own vault (opened in the same transaction if needed), the rate, the amount owed on the due date (labelled as the figure with no pause, since a pause moves the date and interest keeps running), the grace period, the interest mode and schedule, every lifecycle term the signature binds even when it is the default (partial repayment, selling the collateral during or before the loan, any refinance or linked loan), the lender, and whether a token approval is needed. The consequences follow plainly: default after grace, liquidation or, for each leg that is illiquid, that leg named and the in-kind handover, the lock, the interest commitment, and that signing records a binding agreement. Confirm waits for an explicit consent to the Risk Disclosures and Terms, bound to the figures shown: a re-read that changes them clears it. A wallet short of the collateral is told by how much and cannot confirm. When Terms are in force and not yet accepted, they are asked for first.

The wallet is asked to sign the exact terms first, which costs nothing and moves nothing; then, only if the allowance falls short, to approve exactly the collateral locked; then the acceptance is dry-run with the real signature and sent only if the protocol would accept it. The allowance is read again before the first prompt, and if anything stops the acceptance after an approval, including a sent acceptance that reverts because someone took the offer first, the app asks to put the approval back to that value and says whether that happened. The outcome is measured from the acceptance's own transaction: the loan it opened for this borrower, the amount that arrived in the wallet, checked to add up with the rest of the fee to the principal the loan records, and the collateral that moved into the vault, each against the review's figures, with anything the logs do not show reported as unknown.

Posting one's own borrow request, NFT collateral, pair consents and the Advanced options come in the next Borrow updates; the page says so instead of hiding them. The user guide's Basic and Advanced editions gain Borrow sections in every language.
