## Thread — a reward budget can arrive committed to its days by a root and be held whole as its own epoch (#1566 transport epochs 3d-1, the d6 wire's receive side)

**Problem.** Every remittance wire so far carried the list of days it funded in the message itself. That list is what the destination writes, day by day, into the indexes every draw reads — and a message is immutable, so a list too wide for one call to write cannot be refused at the destination without refusing the same message for ever. The transport-epoch design closed that for the OLD wires with the compact admission: the arrival is recorded whole and the list is re-supplied and re-hashed, page by page, afterwards. For the new wire it asked for something stronger — a Merkle root over fixed-size indexed chunks of the list, together with the list's extent, so each materialization call verifies one bounded chunk against the root instead of re-hashing the whole list, and the chain knows exactly when the membership is whole. It also asked that the packet carry the era it was dispatched in, because the coming era-registry change validates a delivery's intended era at the receive ingress and no wire before this one carries one.

**What changed — the receive side.** A sixth payload shape, d6, is decoded by the mirror-side receiver: in place of the day list it carries the root, the element count, the chunk count, the total, the remittance id, the sender's address, the recycled share and the intended era. The receiver forwards it through a new Diamond ingress. On a deployment whose reward custody is activated, that ingress records the packet, stamps its commitment and extent and era, takes the split the wire stated as the packet's attested caps — the wire is the attestation; a later one from the canonical chain finds the same figures and is held to them — and opens the delivery's transport epoch at once, holding the WHOLE delivery: nothing of it is credited to the live delivered ledger or the recycle bucket at ingress, and the days the root commits to draw it first under the rules every other epoch obeys (transport-first, the contested and necessity gates, staging), with only the residual reaching the shared sources. A wire whose extent is not the fixed-size chunking of its element count, that commits to nothing, or that declares more chunks than the index can mark is refused by name; so is a split that does not account for the whole delivery, because the whole delivery becomes one epoch typed by exactly two caps and a wei under neither would be value no day could draw.

**Chunks, in any order, each once.** Anyone may index a rooted epoch's membership one proven chunk at a time: the chunk's index must lie inside the committed extent and not have landed before, its length must be the one the extent commits to for that index, and its indexed leaf must verify against the root under the supplied proof. Each chunk's days then go through the same per-day writer a flat-hash page uses — one implementation of "this batch lists this day" for both commitments. The epoch's index count reaches its day count exactly when every chunk has landed, and that count is what every allocation and the park already gate on, so a rooted epoch is spendable, and its remainder parkable, only when whole — by the existing rule rather than a new one. Parking a rooted epoch reads the list its chunks wrote; no list is re-supplied, and a supplied one is refused by name. The flat-hash page refuses a rooted batch by name, and the chunk route refuses a flat-hash one.

**Before activation.** On a deployment whose custody is not yet activated a rooted delivery takes the typed-ledger path exactly as a d5 delivery does — the fresh component counted, the recycled one credited to the bucket — and opens no epoch, for the reason the untyped path already states: the activation is what assigns tokens that rest with the Diamond, and an epoch beside it would be two records on one sum. Its commitment and era are still stamped.

**The send side is unchanged, and now has one encoder.** Every live lane keeps speaking d5; moving the sender to d6 and lifting the per-remittance day cap is the next change (3d-2). From this change on the send and its fee quote build the d5 payload through one shared encoder, so the quote prices the bytes the send emits by construction. The receiver's wire generation advances to 6, so the in-place refresh upgrades every deployed receiver rather than reading one as current.

**What this changes in accounting, and what is recorded for the owner.** Under this reading of the design a new remittance no longer tops up the live delivered ledger or the recycle bucket at ingress; the live ledgers keep what they hold and the claim's live leg shrinks to what the epochs cannot cover. That is the design's stated order ("an obligation on a day a delivery listed is paid from that delivery's epoch first"), and the alternative — a root riding a ledger-credit wire — would leave the root, the extent and the chunked index with nothing to gate. Both readings, the chunk size (sixteen days, half an index page) and the chunk-count bound are recorded on #2388 for the owner before 3d-2 switches a live lane. A rooted delivery wider than the per-remittance fan-out cap is accepted and indexed but withheld as contested by the gate change (3c-1), which reads a batch's listed days on the hot path and declines a list it cannot read; the cap lift waits on a gate that reads a wide list off that path.
