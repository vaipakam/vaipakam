## Thread — Public rate curve: history and public read (indexer)

The indexer now publishes a rate curve for each lending asset at 7, 30, 90, 180 and 365 days, through two public reads: the current curve and one point's daily history. Each answer carries two halves side by side and labelled apart: the outside-source term average the rate reference reports, and a figure built from the platform's own loans. The two are never blended into one number, and every answer says the figures are observed averages, not advice, a forecast or an offer.

The platform's own figure follows the standing rule in TermAverageAndLinkGuardsDesign.md §8, with the settings the design thread gave: every loan whatever its status, each weighted by the interest actually paid on it so far (read from the per-loan interest statements below), self-trades, unconfirmed terms and loans under three days old left out, one wallet capped at a fifth of the weight on each side, at least five distinct lenders and five distinct borrowers per point, and a quartile band rather than a mean. Each point covers a stated range of loan durations and looks back over the larger of 90 days and twice its term. Every setting is stated in each answer, so the page's "how this is computed" note reads them rather than restating them.

An hourly pass writes one row per asset, point and day into a new history table, recomputing today and yesterday and filling up to seven missing earlier days each run, so the history rebuilds itself after a restore or a fresh deploy. The reads only read stored rows, are cached for five minutes, and sit behind a new per-caller request limit that refuses when it cannot count.

The outside-source half reports "not published" on every deployment for now: the rate reference's term-average view (#3713 with #3337 part 1) is not built yet. When it is, the pass reads it into the same columns and the answer's shape does not change.

The weight reads the record the contracts now emit for every interest payment on a loan (#3755): one record per payment, and a take-back record when a payment parked for the lender side arrived short. The indexer keeps each record per loan in a new table, once per log, so the interest a loan has paid up to any day is its payments less its take-backs up to then. Every route that pays interest counts the same way, including partial repayments, refinance payoffs and repayment with collateral, which the first version could not read; so the first version's limit to repaid loans is gone: every loan counts by what it has paid so far, open or closed, and a loan that paid interest and then defaulted or was liquidated keeps that weight (design ruling of 2026-11-19), since a status filter would move the band for a reason that is not a rate and lean it toward loans that ended well. A deployment older than #3755 emits no such record, so its loans weigh nothing and its points read "not enough loans".
> Caveat: all settings are provisional and await real readings.
