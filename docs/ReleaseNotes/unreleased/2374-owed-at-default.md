## Thread — the protocol records what a loan owed when it defaulted, and a lender's claim shows the shortfall against it (PR #TBD)

Until now, nothing on chain kept what a loan owed at the moment it defaulted, so the connected app could only say that a lender's default recovery could not be compared with anything. The loan's current principal was not an honest substitute: partial repayments change it, and a holder who bought the lender position on the secondary market never lent it at all. The protocol now records that figure itself. When an ERC-20 loan is liquidated, defaults on time, or enters the full-collateral fallback because its collateral could not be sold, the protocol stores, at that block and in the loan's principal asset, the principal outstanding, the interest outstanding net of anything already settled, and the late fee. This is the same basis the forced close settles its debt on. It also notes whether the default went through the fallback. The figure is computed once, at the single place every default status is written, so the forced-close paths themselves are unchanged. That matters because several of them have almost no contract-size room left. A public read returns the record, and an event announces it.

Entering the fallback is treated as the default. When the fallback loan later moves to defaulted at the lender's claim, the figure from the entry is kept rather than restated at the claim's time, because interest kept running in between. A fallback that the borrower cures by adding collateral, or repays in full, loses its record: the loan did not in the end default. NFT rentals get no record, because their "principal" is a daily fee and the lender's claim is the NFT itself. Neither do loans fully closed by internal matching, nor loans that defaulted before this change.

On a defaulted lender claim, the app now states what the loan still owed when it defaulted, with principal and charges shown separately. This is phrased for whoever holds the position now, never as "you lent". It compares the recovery only against the principal, because the protocol takes no share of principal. A recovery below the principal is shown as a shortfall of that amount. Above it, the app says the principal is covered in full and does not count the remaining interest and late fees as a loss, since those are paid after the protocol takes its share. When the recovery may have come in more than one part — proceeds held beside the claim, a claim in another asset, or a default that went through the fallback — the figure is stated without a comparison. When there is no record, the earlier "not available" line stays. When the record cannot be read, the app says that instead. The copy is translated in every supported language. Closes #2374.
