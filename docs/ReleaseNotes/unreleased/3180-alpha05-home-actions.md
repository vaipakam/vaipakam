# The next app's Home can sign: test assets, the fee discount, and the Terms

Home in the next connected app gains its first actions, and with them the one signing flow every later page will use. Before the wallet opens, a receipt states what the action does, what stays unchanged, what the reader can lose and how many wallet prompts it takes. Right before the wallet opens, the inputs are read again and the call is tried against the chain: a change reopens the review, the protocol's own refusal is shown as given, and a check that cannot be made withholds signing. While the transaction runs, the wallet step and its progress are narrated; afterwards the outcome is read back from the chain and reported as done, not done, partly done or unknown, never inferred from the receipt alone. A transaction whose receipt cannot be read keeps its hash on screen and can be checked again; nothing new is offered until it is.

On a test network whose deployment record publishes test assets, Home offers them: test tokens with a test price, test tokens with none, and rentable test NFTs, each badged as a test asset with no value. Each mint says that the test token contract mints it, not the protocol, and the result is measured from the balance or the NFT's owner. A finished mint offers the next jobs and, for a token, to show it in the wallet.

The fee-discount setting can now be turned on and off from Home, as the platform's one per-network flag. The review says what changes for borrowing and for lending, and how a lender's discount is delivered on that network (paid in VPFI from the vault where a price source is configured, otherwise a smaller fee with no VPFI moved). Turning it on asks for the Terms of Service in force first, when a version is in force: the prompt names the action that asked, shows the version, the whole fingerprint of its text, the version's permanent page and the privacy notice, and continues to the setting once accepted. Turning it off never asks. While the protocol is paused the setting cannot be changed in either direction, and Home says so with what stays open.

The rewards widget that was planned beside these moves to the next Home part, with the shared claim flow it needs. The user guide's Basic and Advanced editions gain sections on the test assets, the fee discount and the Terms prompt, in every language.
