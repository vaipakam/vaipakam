## Thread — a dated end for a loan left in the fallback state (PR #<n>)

A loan reaches the fallback state when a liquidation or a time-based default could not sell the collateral through any route. The collateral then waits in the borrower's vault under the loan's lien, split three ways (lender, treasury, borrower), until the lender claims it, the borrower cures, or a later match or retry resolves it. Until now nothing ended that wait if no one acted, so a loan could sit in the fallback state indefinitely, with the borrower's part and the treasury's part held up behind a lender who never claimed. From now on every entry into the fallback state records a date, and once that date has passed anyone may finish the loan in kind.

The date is the entry time plus a governed wait, counted on the loan clock. The wait is 30 days unless governance sets otherwise, within 14 to 90 days, and it changes only through the admin role, which the handover gives to the governance timelock. It is recorded on the loan at entry, so changing the setting never moves a date already running. Paused time does not count, as it counts for no loan deadline, and a date that falls inside the latest sequencer outage or its recovery window moves to the window's end, as a grace period does. A cure ends the count, and a later entry records a fresh date. A top-up that does not cure and a part internal match leave the date where it was.

Finishing a loan in kind does what the lender's claim without a swap route already does, and stops before anyone is paid. The recorded split is applied as it stands (it is never worked out again from a price): the treasury's part goes to the treasury, the lender's part goes into the lender's vault to wait for the lender's claim, and the borrower's part stays in the borrower's vault, together with any top-up that did not cure, as the borrower's claim. The loan becomes Defaulted, both position tokens read Loan Defaulted until their claims close them, and the lender's request to be bought out by the backstop lapses. Nothing is sold, matched or priced, the caller is paid nothing, and no wallet receives anything. A flagged holder does not hold the step up: both holders go under the usual sanctions hold, and a flagged holder's part stays locked until its own claim clears the screen. A token that refuses the lender's vault is parked for the lender side and released by the lender's claim, as a refused repayment leg already is. The step stays open while a network is closing and refuses a legacy Diamond-held fallback row by name.

A new view reports a loan's date (entry time, recorded wait, the date as it stands now, and whether the step would run now), reporting nothing for a loan that is not in the fallback state under a live snapshot, so a record left by a cured episode is never shown as current. Events mark the entry's date and the finish. The connected app's Activity feed labels both (in English and its nine translated languages), and the shared activity rules count whoever finished the loan as that event's actor.

The keeper pass that calls the step on loans whose date has passed, the alerts before the date, the indexer's handling of the finish event and the loan page's display of the date are owed by their own threads; the step is listed as a recorded gap in the permissionless-step ledger until the keeper pass lands.

Advances #3865.

> Caveat: if a refused lender payment is already parked for the same loan, the finish reverts rather than parking a second one; the lender's claim releases the first park.
> Caveat: a token that refuses the treasury reverts the finish, as it reverts the lender's claim today.
> Caveat: the curated facet refresh does not carry the settlement-leg facet the finish uses for its park; only the finish fails there, by a missing route.
