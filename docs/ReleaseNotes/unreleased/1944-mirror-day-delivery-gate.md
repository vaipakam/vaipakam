## Thread — a mirror day that owes fresh funding waits for its own proven delivery (#1944)

**Problem.** On a mirror chain, the canonical chain's day broadcast is what opens that day's claims, and the budget remittance that funds the day travels as a separate message. Either can arrive first, and the broadcast is permissionless — anyone paying the transport fee can send a finalized day. A broadcast that landed before its budget opened claims against funding that had not arrived. The original bridge design treated that as a harmless "revert and retry". It was not: the mirror's balance also holds value that belongs to other owners, borrower collateral among it, so an early claim could be paid out of it. The only defence was the governance runbook's containment procedure — keep the reward messenger paused for the whole mesh — which could not be scoped to one mirror.

**What changed.** A mirror can now hold any day that owes it fresh funding until a budget delivery proven to list that day has landed there, whichever of the two messages arrives first. Until then the chain's reward cursor stops before the day, and every settlement path defers and moves no value: the claim, the windowed claim, per-loan entry settlement, both expiry sweeps, staging, domain preparation and the previews. A claim with nothing else to pay is refused with an error that names the day, so it is no longer reported as "nothing to claim". Whether a day owes fresh funding is read from what the canonical chain itself stamped and reported for that chain and day. So a day the canonical chain sends nothing for never waits: a destination excluded from the day, a side with no interest on that chain, an armed side whose reported liability is zero, or a side funded only from recycled value. Anyone may prove a delivery. A delivery that already carries indexed transport epochs counts as soon as that day's chunk is indexed. Any other budget delivery is proven by presenting its day list, or one rooted chunk of it, against the commitment it arrived with. Nothing is written per day when a delivery arrives, so the arrival keeps its existing gas budget. A proof records which delivery first listed the day and emits an event. It is provenance only and moves no value. A delivery quarantined to an older custody era, or one already disposed of, cannot prove a day. The same quarantine now also stops the existing rollout admission path: until now a quarantined legacy delivery could reach it, against the arrival's own stated rule.

**Ships dark.** The gate is a one-shot, mirror-only switch that an administrator sets to the first day it binds. Until it is set, the pricing order is exactly what it was. Arm it on a mirror only once the keeper's delivery-proof pass runs there (its own entry). A mirror whose custody era has moved may arm it too: since the custody-era revision every remittance carries the destination's own era and a retired era's delivery opens its epoch, so nothing is held inert. The switch refuses a first day that the reward cursor has already passed, because those days were priced without the gate. New read views give operators a day's delivery state and a delivery's proof state. One structural part is not behind the switch: the expiry sweep now runs its transfer test through the interaction-rewards read facet, so the reward facets must be refreshed together on every chain; a partial refresh stops expiry sweeps until that read facet is updated.

**Not dark — a pricing fix that applies on every mirror now.** A mirror now prices every day the canonical chain stamped from that stamp, whether the day is armed or not. Before, an unarmed (pre-cutover) day was priced from the fixed schedule. For an included day the two figures are the same, so nothing changes there. They differ in two cases, and in both the old pricing was wrong. First, a day the mirror was excluded from now pays zero on that mirror instead of the schedule. Second, a day stamped at a reduced floor because the fresh allocation was running out now pays that floor instead of the schedule. In both cases the mirror used to pay against funding the day never received. The canonical chain's own pricing is unchanged. Because this fix is not behind the switch, the reward suites that deliver real broadcasts to a mirror are re-run rather than assumed green.

**Limits, stated.** With the gate armed, availability depends on proofs. Every included day waits for its remittance and its proof, and armed days also wait for the chain's commitment report. A stalled keeper therefore produces named deferrals, never a loss. There is deliberately no administrative release on the mirror. A day whose delivery can never execute is repaired on the canonical chain by releasing the reservation, which needs seven days at the earliest, and remitting again. After arming, a change of the mirror's reward role moves its custody era and quarantines every later delivery until the era registry lands, so the cutover runbook forbids that change on an armed mirror. These cases stay outside the gate: days applied only by the legacy broadcast that carries no stamp; days before the gate's first day; any mirror whose gate is dark (the runbook's containment still applies to it); a late inclusion backfill on the canonical chain; and a delivery that has landed but cannot be drawn yet because it is not whole and attested, which the delivered bound governs. The mirror's rule is kept in step with the canonical chain's send rule through stored figures rather than shared code, and a mesh test pins that the two agree. Refs #1944 (consequence 1. The remedy, proving the day's own delivery, is new relative to the menu recorded on the issue and is recorded for the owner's ratification).
