# The next app has a Home page

Home is the first page of the next connected app to be built. It reads; nothing on it signs.

With no wallet connected, it opens on a choice of job: borrow, lend, rent an NFT, rent out an NFT, or manage what you have. The choice comes before any protocol term, and each card opens the guided page for that job. With a wallet connected, three short cards introduce the app the first time (your own vault, a receipt before every signature, and help in place); each can be dismissed and stays dismissed in that browser.

Below them is the wallet's book on its network, read from the chain through the wallet's own connection. A position belongs to whoever holds its NFT, so the book is built from the position NFTs the wallet holds, not from the parties recorded when each loan began, and an offer counts as open only when the protocol's own state says so (not cancelled, expired, accepted or sold). An answer kept after a failed re-read is labelled with its age. Payouts ready to collect come first, named per loan and side, with their amounts left to the Claims page, which reads every way a payout is paid. Live loans follow, the most urgent deadline first: each states its side, terms, collateral, health factor and next date in plain words. A borrower close to the due date, in the grace period, or past it is told the consequence. Loans opened from one offer are shown together with the principal filled, the principal-weighted rate and the lowest health factor among them. A headline compares what the wallet has lent with what it has borrowed at today's prices, and names the larger side only when loans that cannot be valued could not change the answer. The wallet's open offers come last. Each loan's button opens the loan, where the shared eligibility rule decides which actions are open. A section that could not be read says so, and "nothing yet" is said only when every list was read in full.

The protocol's pause posture is stated on every visit. A paused protocol, or a paused keeper service, is stated at the top with what stays open; a running protocol is one quiet line at the end. A sync line says how long ago the wallet's book was read and through which source.

The user guide's Basic and Advanced editions gain a chapter on the next app in every language, which the page's "Why?" links open. It also covers the modes, where the app's own figures come from and how it reads what the deployment supports, whose "Why?" links had pointed at sections the guide did not have yet. The test-scenario harness can now drive a scenario as a suite's own per-run wallets, and its lender offer can be posted without being accepted.
