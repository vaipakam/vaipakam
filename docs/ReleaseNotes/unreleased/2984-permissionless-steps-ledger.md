## Contracts — every permissionless step is recorded with what backs it (PR #3098)

The owner's rule is that every permissionless step that keeps the protocol moving has the platform keeper as its backstop. Until now nothing recorded which open entry point was which, so a new step could ship with no caller and stall silently. The new ledger, `contracts/permissionless-steps.json`, lists every state-changing entry point anyone may call on the Diamond and on the contracts shipped beside it, and says what backs each one: a keeper pass and its arming flag, a keeper pass still in review, another committed service (the sanctions delister), another route to a step already backed, the party it serves, a one-time initializer, a caller restriction stated in words for review, or a recorded gap with the issue that decides it.

Whether an entry point is open is read from the compiler's syntax tree, not claimed. A function is caller-gated when, before it can finish, it reverts unless the caller is a particular address, holds a role or appears in a registry. The reading follows modifiers, internal helpers (with the caller passed in), virtual overrides (so an upgradeable contract's owner-only upgrade is seen as gated), if/else branches and early exits only an allowed caller can take. It does not follow external calls, whose caller is the contract itself; it does not count a comparison of the caller with an address the caller passed in (a role renunciation's confirmation, a protected-account setter named by its own account), since every caller can pass its own address; and it treats a KYC requirement as a screen rather than a gate, because it admits everyone while enforcement is off. Of 797 state-changing entry points, 622 are caller-gated and 175 are open; all 175 are classified.

The pre-deploy gate runs the full check as step 1e, reading the same syntax-tree build as the measured-delivery and loan-terms guards. A keeper row's sending file must carry the function's name as a string literal in code; a comment, a backticked mention or an ABI fragment's name does not count. The keeper's typecheck runs the keeper half on its own, so a keeper change that stops sending a recorded step, or renames its pass or flag, fails there too. A keeper-pending row is refused once the keeper already sends the call, so a merged pass must update its row.

Seventeen steps are recorded as gaps (staging continuations and unwinds, the daily NFT-rental deduction, the backstop fill, tier-exclusion refresh, expired-intent and expired prepay-listing teardown, claim consolidation, stale-sale teardown, the sanctions-flag refresh and reward-entry compaction). Each needs an owner call between a keeper backstop and the party it serves; that is #3097. Closes #2984.

> Caveat: a caller restriction the reading cannot see is recorded as "gated" with a written reason; eight such rows exist (signature-relayed risk settings, the delisting veto, the governance timelock's executor role), and a reviewer audits them rather than the check.
> Caveat: when the delivery-proof pass is retired (#3064) and the delivery-conflict and protected-account passes merge (#2999, #3090), their ledger rows must change in the same change; the check fails until they do.
