## Thread — transport epochs 3b-ii-A2-i: a deferred claim day is staged, not abandoned (PR #2308)

Until this change, a claim day that more epochs listed than one read could scan was deferred with nothing drawn: the coverage the window had found was released back to the epochs, and the next attempt started over against the same live batches. Now such a day keeps what it found. The window's coverage moves into a staging record for that claimant's day — debited from the epochs, credited to no one — and anyone may continue staging the rest from where the last scan stopped, reserve the day's residual from the live sources once staged transport plus the live sources can cover it, resolve the record page by page, and be paid on the last page. Nothing settles before that last page: the day pays out, persists and exhausts its epochs only then, and until then nothing is half-paid. A record that stalls expires on a deadline sized from the work its day's list needs, and past it anyone may unwind it, returning exactly what it held; the claimant may cancel earlier. A record that has begun resolving is beyond any deadline and only completes.

Every value movement is explicit. An epoch's staged components are recorded on the epoch and named in an event with the record; the live recycled residual and, page by page, the epoch legs are held in a dedicated custody row against the record; fresh and the cap headroom are reserved by count — the live fresh row's era ledger records every debit as a spend, so fresh is never held out of it — with the figures written on the record so the unwind reverses each source exactly. Reserved headroom reads as encumbered, never as paid: a concurrent one-call settlement sees less pool, less delivered headroom, less recycled runway and less loan-side cap, and defers rather than treating the reservation as a payout — so a loan side another holder's record has reserved is never trimmed against the reservation, and an entry's expiry clock does not run through an interval in which its owner's claim would defer on one. A record stages exactly what a claim may draw: an attested delivery's epoch whose membership is whole and that lists only the day being settled. An unattested delivery's epoch is neither drawn nor staged, and a record remembers each one it passes and re-checks it before any live source is reserved, so an attestation landing after the scan is never lost. An epoch listing more than one day is withheld until the contested-allocation machinery lands; a record's scan passes it without counting it as pending, so a day holding many of them never fills the record's page of re-checks. When a record gives staged value back to an epoch — by unwinding, or by returning what it staged beyond what its reservation assigned — the return is logged for the day and announced, and every other record standing on that day re-offers the epoch before it may reserve anything from the live sources: a record that scanned the epoch while it was drained would otherwise reserve live funding past it. The record's own scan is the only scan that advances its place in the day's list: the read that opened it records none, so the record's first preparation rescans that read's window, one page, and disposes of every epoch it passed. Whoever indexes a delivery may name its predecessor in a day's late chain as well as in the day's list, so a chain deeper than the bounded walk covers can still be joined in constant work. A record's scan offers each epoch once, even when a restarted chain walk meets an epoch its re-checks already offered, and a restart counts its own pages in the record's deadline. What a record has reserved of the live fresh is unavailable to the reconciliation moves as well — the compensation demotion and the fresh-to-recycled correction leave it in the row and on the delivered ledger — so a resolving record's last page can always charge and pay what it reserved. The reservations themselves, and what each source has available net of them, are published by the rewards lens, so an operator can see why a settlement defers and how much custody stands committed; a record's committed entries with their reserved slices, and the epochs it passed over pending, are readable page by page beside its epochs. A claim that names where it is to be delivered carries that venue into any record it opens, so the staged part is delivered where the paid part was. The claimant's expiry clock does not run while a record holds their day. A record that passes more pending epochs than it tracks is prepared no further and can only be unwound, so it references nothing more for a day it can never settle. The in-place refresh retires the earlier shapes of the entries whose signatures changed. The public reward preview reports nothing at all while a reservation would make the claim defer, the legacy window's part included, as the claim itself pays nothing. Reward custody cannot be activated while a staging record is resolving, since a resolving record's consumed epoch value rests in the Diamond's balance under no attribution until its last page pays it; the record's remaining pages are anyone's to drive, and it only completes. The cooldown a release puts on an obligation is published — in an event at the release and through a read on the epoch view — so a claim that opens no record during it is never an unexplained silence. A record's page of remembered pending epochs is reconciled before it is enforced, so a full page that frees places by re-checking does not overflow on the epochs it takes in the same scan; and the preview's availability test counts a forfeited legacy entry's fresh once, as the claim does. A record asks its day's epochs for no more fresh than the pool could ever pay, so a pool that shrinks while a record scans stops further staging without stopping the scan. The custody activation ceremony reads the count of resolving records before it sends anything, and the epoch view publishes that count; the lens publishes the live fresh available beside the reserved; and a cooldown that has passed reads as none. Every restart of a record's late-chain walk adds the chain it re-walks to the record's deadline, not only the first. What staging records have reserved of the recycled runway is out of every mover's reach on the recycled side as well: a remittance plans from the runway net of it, a repatriation and a reattribution may take only what is fundable net of it, and a settlement can never consume below it — three figures that each carried their own copy of the commitment subtraction now read one. The expiry clocks read the same fresh room the claim's gate reads, so a reservation of the live fresh pauses them as it defers the claim. A record's resolution and unwind consume its epochs in the order the plan staged them and drop each as they go, so a consumption budget that runs out partway spends the epochs the plan ranked first and returns the rest, and closing a record that accumulated many epochs over many preparations never costs more than one page: the epochs a record holds are not kept inside the record itself. Value in staged form is an earmark of the Diamond's own balance while reward custody is inactive, so no other claim can spend what a record is holding: the share a page returns to its epoch leaves that earmark with it, and the share a page consumes stays in it until the last page pays the claimant. The earmark is published beside the reservations, so the figure that lowers the available balance is never unexplained. And a record holding a reservation blocks every change of posture, not only the custody activation: a reward-role change moves the delivered allowance the record's payout will charge, so it waits for the record to settle or unwind — both of which anyone may drive.

To make room for this on the settle path, the claim's entry walk, the expiry sweep's walk and the forfeit sweep's walk moved off the facets that inlined them onto three hosts reached by one self-call each; the facets keep their facades and their own accounting, and the staging lifecycle has a facet of its own with its reads beside the epochs' other reads. Scope: standalone claim days. A sweep's day, a multi-chunk domain and the legacy slice are the next slice (#2305). Refs #1566, #1956, #2305, #2296; design in #2297.
