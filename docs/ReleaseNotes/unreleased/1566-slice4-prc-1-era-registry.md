## Thread — a chain's reward role lives in eras: a role change carries the delivered custody into the retiring era instead of levelling it away (#1566 slice 4 PR C-1)

**Problem.** When a chain changed its reward role, the delivered-fresh residual was retired by levelling the paid counter up to the received one. That erred safe — the chain resumed with no headroom — but it left the custody that residual backed sitting in the live row with nothing that could spend it, and it recorded no history: after two transitions nobody could say what the first era had delivered, what it still owed, or when it ended. The design's answer is an era registry: the span of a role between two changes is an era; a change retires the live era and opens the next; what the retiring era delivered stays with it, consumable by the obligations accrued in it and released only when it is terminal.

**What changed.** The custody era registry exists: the live era's id, and per era its holder row, two terminal counters — how many of its days the per-day closure proof has proven closed, and how many packets stamped into it are not yet finally classified — the day it opened on, and a terminal flag. On an activated custody, every effective role change — any change of the four-state role, not only of the mirror predicate — retires the live era: the live-fresh row moves, inside the holder, into the retiring era's holder row; the delivered counters are levelled so the new baseline opens with no headroom; a deficit the old baseline owed is not erased but recorded as the new era's opening deficit, which the first credit of the new era covers first under the deficit split; and the next era opens with today's reward day as its boundary. Era rows join the attributed total, so the invariant that attributed custody never exceeds the holder's balance now covers them. Before activation the registry is untouched and the counters are levelled as before, so the history backfill will find every pre-activation figure in the first era.

**Two attribution writers, dark.** The era-terminal release finalizes a retired era that is free of outstanding liability — every day of its span proven closed by the existing per-day closure proof, which anyone may run, and so counted on the roomy closure facet rather than written on the loan path — and whose packets are all finally classified; it releases its row — to the live baseline under the deficit split on an active role, into the pending-surplus row while the chain is detached (never into recovery). The pending-surplus drain credits that row to the live baseline once an active era exists. Both are deterministic bookkeeping over custody the holder already holds, so both are permissionless, and both share the one fresh-credit rule every ingress uses. Both refuse by name until the history backfill has assigned past packets to eras: until then a zero packet count means "not yet counted", not "nothing owed". The closed-day count is live from this slice; the packet count is written by the ingress and the backfill in the next slices.

**What stays as it was, and why.** Role changes stay frozen on an activated custody, and every custody credit still re-arms that freeze: the freeze means "the holder carries value and the history is not yet assigned", and only the backfill's completion lifts it. The per-era decomposition of a claim's need, the ingress-side era validation, the parked lane for era-less broadcasts and the backfill are the following slices of this programme.
