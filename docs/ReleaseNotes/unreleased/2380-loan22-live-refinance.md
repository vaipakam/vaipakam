## Thread — A real illiquid-collateral loan refinanced through the live app; indexer ingest restored (PR #2422)

Loan 22 on Base Sepolia was refinanced end to end through app.vaipakam.com on 5 October, using the dev test wallets. That loan is backed by illiquid collateral that both parties consented to, and it is the loan whose refinance used to fail (#2380). The borrower posted the refinance request from the loan's page (request #45, 12% for 30 days). A different lender funded it through the app's own review-and-sign steps. All five transactions succeeded, and each wallet's transaction count rose by exactly the transactions the drive saw. Their contents were later compared field by field with the requests the committed drive now expects, with no differences.

What the run checked on chain. Request #45 targeted loan 22 by carry-over, at the typed rate ceiling and length, for the old principal and collateral. Loan 22 closed as repaid, and loan 23 opened from that request for the same borrower and the accepting lender, on the same collateral and principal, still marked illiquid with both parties' consent recorded, at the requested rate and length. The borrower's balance of the collateral token never moved.

What was checked after the run, against the same blocks. These checks were added to the committed drive during review, which now makes them itself. Reading the chain at the block before the accept and at the accept block showed the collateral lien released and zeroed on loan 22 and live, with the same borrower, asset and amount, on loan 23. The same reads showed the settlement matching the contract's own payoff figure to the smallest unit. The old lender's claim and vault rose by the payoff less the 2% treasury share of its interest. The borrower received the new principal less the 0.2% initiation fee and paid the payoff. The accepting lender paid the principal and received the 1% matcher share of the fee. The transaction's own token transfers show the treasury receiving its share of the interest and of the fee. The borrower's vault still held its collateral, and the accept moved none of it. The accept block's other 133 transactions touched neither the protocol nor any participant, so those before-and-after differences belong to the accept alone. The settlement model applied because, at that block, the borrower's effective fee discount was zero and the exiting lender had no discount entitlement. The settlement check now relies only on protocol state, not on whether the principal token currently counts as liquid. If the borrower has any fee discount, the drive says up front that the settlement will not be verified. The figures on both review screens were also compared against what the app rendered during the run, and they match the chain.

What this drive does not check. It does not cover a refinance accepted inside the grace window, an exiting lender's yield-fee discount or a borrower's initiation-fee discount, a loan with liquid collateral, the old lender claiming the payout, how the indexer and the app's lists show the request and the new loan afterwards, or reward accounting. It also does not check whether an approval is left standing. The app approves the payoff for the request's last fillable moment, so an earlier accept leaves the difference approved; this run left about 0.0000764 WETH. The drive's verdict is now a list of the claims it verified, each with the chain reads behind it, and the claims it did not, each with where it is covered. It replaces the single "all checks passed" line. The exact-settlement check covers only the default fee posture, and the drive checks that posture before it writes anything. If a discount could apply, it says before the first write that the settlement will not be verified. If, after the write, a check's premise no longer holds, the drive marks it undetermined and says why. This happens when the fee posture changed by the accept, or when another transaction in the same block touched the same state. Undetermined is never reported as a failure. The same discipline now applies everywhere the drive compares the page or a transaction with the chain. A figure on screen is judged only against configuration that stayed unchanged around the moment it was read. What one of the run's transactions did is read from that transaction's own receipt, or from before-and-after state only when nothing else in its block touched the same accounts. If another party fills the run's request, the failure report counts the spent payoff approval as this run's doing and says how to restore it. The payoff approval may no longer exceed what the request could cost at its own last fillable moment. Every reading taken after a write is tied to that transaction's block. The old lender's payout is checked against the account the protocol actually pays. That is normally whoever holds the lender position, and the drive says so when the two differ. A new version of the risk terms appearing mid-run stops the drive before the lender signs. Every protocol setting the drive relies on is now defined once and watched together: the fees, the treasury address, the grace periods, the risk terms, the pause switches and the per-asset pauses. If any of them changed between the reviews and the accept, the settlement is reported as undetermined rather than verified. The fee rates stamped on the new loan are checked against the rates the reviews showed. On loan 23 they matched: 2% treasury fee and 0.2% initiation fee. Balance changes are now checked per account, so the check stays correct when two roles share a vault. The drive now states which kinds of loan it supports, field by field. It covers full-term interest only, with no periodic interest, no already-settled interest, ERC-20 on both sides and illiquid collateral, and it refuses any other loan before writing anything. A pro-rata loan is one example. A cancelled request no longer counts as open. If someone else fills the request first, the drive reports that and the resulting loan instead of failing. It also checks that the request's stored expiry is exactly what was submitted, cut back to the loan's grace deadline when it ran past it. Request #45 was cut back that way. Two final rules close out the review. First, any failure after the drive has written something goes through one decision. If the drive's own transaction went through, the result is still verified on chain, and the page's failure is recorded separately. Otherwise the drive re-checks everything it relies on: protocol settings, every participant's sanctions screening, the request and the loan. If something changed, it reports what changed instead of blaming the app. Second, every field of the new loan is now checked against a declared source: a term the parties signed, a value carried from the old loan, a reviewed fee, or the moment of acceptance. The few fields that are not checked are listed with the reason. Loan 23 matches on every checked field. The drive is committed as a reusable live check, and a rerun against loan 22 now stops before sending anything because the loan is closed.

The drive turned up two things, each filed. First, the lender's review never says the request is a refinance: it does not mention that accepting also pays off and closes the borrower's existing loan, and it describes the carried-over collateral as newly locked (#2421). Second, the production indexer had stopped advancing at 05:35 UTC. The merge that added the offer collateral-range columns was published automatically without its database migration being applied, and the safety check added the same day correctly held all indexing until the schema caught up. The two pending migrations, both of which only add columns, were applied by hand at 09:02 UTC, and indexing resumed on its next scan with no redeploy; it was advancing again by 09:06. Until then, today's new offers and loans were missing from the app's lists. Making the automatic deploy apply migrations first, and alerting when indexing pauses, is tracked in #2420.
